Rodent-Proofing Your Home Before Winter

Apr 2, 2026 · 7 min read

As the weather cools, mice and rats start looking for the same things you want this time of year: warmth, food, and shelter. Unfortunately, your home offers all three. Fall is the season when rodent activity spikes, which makes it the perfect time to seal them out — before a couple of curious mice become a full-blown infestation inside your walls.

Use this seasonal checklist to rodent-proof your home before winter sets in.

Why fall matters most

Rodents don’t hibernate. When outdoor food becomes scarce and temperatures drop, they migrate toward heated buildings. A mouse can squeeze through a gap the size of a dime, and a rat through a hole the size of a quarter. That means the small imperfections most homeowners never notice are wide-open doors to a rodent. Acting in early fall stops them before they nest and reproduce.

Seal every entry point

Exclusion — physically blocking the ways in — is the most effective long-term rodent strategy. Walk the perimeter of your home, inside and out, and look closely for gaps:

  • Around pipes, cables, and utility lines where they enter the house.
  • Gaps beneath exterior doors and garage doors (add door sweeps).
  • Cracks in the foundation and gaps in siding.
  • Vents, soffits, and roof junctions.
  • Around window frames and where the deck meets the house.

Seal small gaps with steel wool packed into the opening and finished with caulk; rodents can chew through foam and plastic, but not steel. Larger holes may need hardware cloth or metal flashing.

Cut off the food supply

A home with easy food is a home worth invading. Make yours unappealing:

  • Store pantry staples like grains, cereal, and pet food in sealed metal or thick plastic containers.
  • Clean up crumbs and spills promptly, and don’t leave dishes out overnight.
  • Take out the trash regularly and use bins with tight lids.
  • Pick up fallen fruit and secure compost outdoors.
  • Avoid leaving pet food out overnight.

Declutter their hiding spots

Rodents love undisturbed clutter where they can nest unseen. Garages, basements, attics, and storage rooms are prime real estate. Store belongings in sealed bins rather than cardboard boxes, keep storage off the floor where possible, and clear out piles of paper, fabric, and packaging. Outdoors, move firewood, lumber, and debris away from the foundation and keep shrubs trimmed back from the walls.

Know the early signs

Even with good prevention, it pays to catch any intruders fast. Watch for:

  • Droppings along walls, in cabinets, or near food.
  • Gnaw marks on packaging, baseboards, or wiring.
  • Scratching or scurrying sounds in walls and ceilings at night.
  • Shredded paper, fabric, or insulation used for nesting.
  • A musty, ammonia-like odor in enclosed spaces.

One mouse in fall can become dozens by spring. Sealing your home before winter is far easier than evicting an established colony later.

When to call a professional

DIY exclusion and sanitation go a long way, but rodents are persistent and resourceful. If you’re already hearing activity in the walls, finding droppings, or struggling to locate every entry point, professional help is worth it. A technician can perform a full inspection, humanely remove the current population, seal entry points you might miss, and sanitize affected areas to remove the scent trails that attract more rodents.

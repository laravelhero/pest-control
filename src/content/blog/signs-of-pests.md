Pests are masters of staying hidden. By the time you actually see a mouse dart across the kitchen or a roach scatter when the light flips on, the problem has usually been building for weeks. The good news? Infestations leave a trail of clues long before they become obvious. Learning to read those early signals is the single best way to stop a minor nuisance from turning into a costly, stressful invasion.

Here are the five most common warning signs our technicians look for during an inspection — and exactly what you should do when you spot them.

## 1. Droppings and smear marks

Droppings are the clearest evidence that pests are sharing your home. Rodent droppings look like small, dark grains of rice and tend to cluster along walls, inside cabinets, and behind appliances. Cockroach droppings resemble coffee grounds or black pepper. You may also notice greasy "smear marks" along baseboards where rodents brush the same path night after night.

If you find droppings, resist the urge to simply sweep them up. Note where they are concentrated — that tells a professional where the pests are nesting and traveling.

## 2. Unexplained damage

Pests chew, gnaw, and burrow. Mice and rats damage wiring, insulation, food packaging, and even wooden furniture. Termites hollow out structural wood from the inside, leaving a paper-thin surface that sounds hollow when tapped. Fabric pests leave small holes in clothing and upholstery.

Damage that you can't easily explain — frayed wires, gnawed boxes, or wood that crumbles — is a strong indicator that something is living and feeding inside your home.

## 3. Sounds in the walls

Homes make noise, but pests make a particular kind. Scratching, scurrying, or gnawing inside walls and ceilings — especially at night — usually means rodents have moved in. You might also hear faint rustling in the attic or under floorboards. Larger infestations can even produce a soft squeaking or thumping.

Because these sounds are loudest when the house is quiet, many homeowners first notice them after going to bed. If you do, mark the time and location; activity patterns help pinpoint the nest.

## 4. Nests, trails, and grease tracks

Many pests build nests from shredded paper, fabric, insulation, and dried plant material, tucked into warm, hidden corners. Ants leave visible trails as they march between a food source and their colony. Where you see one ant, dozens more are usually following an invisible scent path.

Finding a nest is a sign the infestation is established, not just passing through. Treating the nest — rather than the individual pests you see — is the only way to solve the problem for good.

## 5. Strange smells

A persistent, hard-to-place odor is one of the most overlooked warning signs. Mice leave a musty, ammonia-like smell. Large roach populations produce an oily, musty odor. A sweet, sickly scent can signal decay from a pest that has died inside a wall or vent.

If a room smells "off" no matter how much you clean, pests may be the hidden cause.

## What to do next

Spotting one or two of these signs doesn't mean you should panic — but it does mean you should act quickly. Here's a simple plan:

- Document what you see: take photos and note the locations and times of day.
- Remove easy food and water sources — store food in sealed containers and fix leaks.
- Seal obvious entry points like gaps under doors and around pipes.
- Avoid DIY poisons near children and pets; they often scatter pests without solving the source.
- Schedule a professional inspection to identify the species and the root cause.
